Quick guide

You tend to win when

  • Win The buyer mentions NCDMB, local content, or a customs/freight cost headache
  • Win Vendor payment is currently a separate manual step after the PO
  • Win Team is under ~50 people — enterprise tools are visibly overkill
  • Win They want to be live this week, not next quarter

Be upfront when

  • Watch They ask for a reference customer or published case study — you don't have one yet
  • Watch They operate outside Nigeria — the product isn't built for that today
  • Watch They need deep ERP/accounting integration beyond Xero export
  • Watch They're already mid-implementation with an enterprise suite — that's a displacement, not a first purchase
ProcurePro ₦25,000/mo flat · 14-day trial · no card

Full requisition-to-payment procurement built for Nigerian oil & gas and heavy-industry companies — not a horizontal tool with a Nigeria label stuck on it.

What it does

  • Purchase requests → multi-step approvals (configurable by department + amount) → RFQ/vendor comparison → purchase order → shipping/customs tracking → receiving
  • Multi-currency POs with automatic landed cost — freight and customs duty roll up into one Naira figure per order, regardless of the PO's own currency
  • NCDMB local-content percentage and certificate expiry live on the vendor record itself, not a side spreadsheet
  • Department/category budget enforcement with hard and soft blocks
  • 3-way invoice matching against what was ordered and received
  • Direct vendor payouts via Paystack or Flutterwave transfer, plus customer subscription billing via Paystack
  • Full audit log, role-based access (5 roles), org-isolated multi-tenancy

Say this, honestly

The pitch: "Full enterprise procure-to-pay depth, priced and timed for a mid-market Nigerian company — not a stripped-down PO tracker, and not SAP's cost or 12-month rollout."

Where it's genuinely thin today

  • Honest No completed live payment or subscription transaction yet — the flow is built and tested, not yet proven with real money in production
  • Honest No customer testimonials or case studies — nothing to cite as social proof yet
  • Honest Nigeria-only; no multi-country support
  • Honest No ERP integration beyond a Xero CSV export
Enterprise suites $50k–$250k+/yr · 9–18mo rollout

SAP Ariba, GEP SMART, Coupa. The "we need something serious" instinct that's usually solving for a company 10x the buyer's actual size.

Where they win

  • Brand recognition and analyst validation (Gartner, Forrester) — feels like the safe, defensible choice to a risk-averse buyer
  • Massive global supplier networks and deep ERP ecosystems
  • Built for genuinely complex, multi-entity, multi-country operations — Oilserv (a real Sub-Saharan African oil & gas services company) selected GEP for exactly this reason

Where you win

  • They can be live and using it inside a trial period, not after a 9–18 month implementation
  • No dedicated implementation team required — no naira-based procurement customization needed, it's native
  • Same governance and audit trail without the enterprise price tag or timeline
Talk track: If they're evaluating Ariba/GEP/Coupa, they're likely over-scoped for their actual size. Ask how many active vendors and how large the procurement team is — under ~200 vendors and a lean team, that's over a year of implementation for capability they won't use for years.
Landmine question: "How long did the last implementation like this take at your company, and who was dedicated to it full-time?"
"We need enterprise-grade audit and security."
ProcurePro has full audit logging, row-level-security-isolated multi-tenancy, and role-based access out of the box — the controls exist without the enterprise timeline or cost.
Nigeria-local tools Custom quote (unpublished)

ZYNO Procurement (by EliteMindz), Tradeboss, SeamlessProcure. The closest positioning threat — genuinely local, genuinely worth taking seriously.

Where they win

  • Local support and Lagos-based teams (Tradeboss specifically markets this)
  • SeamlessProcure claims multi-country reach — Nigeria, Ghana, and Kenya
  • Positioned as fast to implement — "weeks, not months" (Tradeboss's own claim)
  • Generic "compliance tools aligned with Nigerian procurement regulations" language (SeamlessProcure)

Where you win

  • None show evidence of oil & gas-specific depth — NCDMB local-content percentage, certificate expiry per vendor, landed cost with customs duty
  • None show evidence the vendor can actually be paid from the product — payment isn't mentioned in any of their public materials
  • ZYNO/EliteMindz's own footprint reads as India-based, publishing Nigeria-targeted content rather than a company built in-region for this vertical
  • None publish pricing — "custom quote" is a real friction point vs. a flat, published ₦25,000
Talk track: These are real, local, worth respecting — not a strawman. But none show the oil & gas vertical depth (NCDMB tracking, landed cost) or built-in vendor payouts. Ask what they use for NCDMB reporting today — if the answer is "a spreadsheet on the side," that's the actual gap.
Landmine questions: "Does it track NCDMB local-content percentage and certificate expiry per vendor?" · "Can it actually pay the vendor, or does it stop at generating the PO?"
"They're cheaper and local."
Get their actual quote in writing — none publish pricing, which itself is worth naming. Then confirm the vertical-depth gap directly rather than competing on "local" alone.
Global mid-market SaaS $35–$2,500+/mo, USD

Precoro, Procurify, Kissflow, Spendflo, ProcureDesk. Mature, polished, genuinely good products — built for a workflow that doesn't include Nigeria.

Where they win

  • Real track records, published customer bases, case studies and reviews on G2/Capterra
  • Polished UX and real ERP/accounting integrations (NetSuite, QuickBooks, Xero)
  • Precoro starts around $35/user/month or $499/mo on the Core plan; ProcureDesk from ~$1,020/mo; Procurify from ~$1,000/mo; Kissflow from ~$2,500/mo

Where you win

  • Zero NCDMB or local-content tracking — not built for this regulatory environment at all
  • No landed-cost/customs-duty modeling — built for domestic-only commerce, not import-heavy equipment purchasing
  • USD per-seat or flat-USD pricing; convert Precoro's per-user cost to Naira for a 10-person team and it often exceeds ProcurePro's flat ₦25,000
  • No Naira-native payment rails for paying a Nigerian vendor directly
Talk track: These are legitimately good products — don't argue "we're better." The honest pitch is "built for a workflow they've never seen": landed cost with customs duty, NCDMB compliance, Naira-native payment. Do the per-user USD-to-Naira math with them live; it's usually the moment the pricing conversation flips.
Landmine question: "How does it handle customs duty and freight cost on an imported PO, and does it track NCDMB compliance per vendor?" — it doesn't; neither concept exists in these products.
"It's a more mature product with more integrations."
True, and worth naming directly. Ask what happens to their landed-cost and NCDMB tracking today — if it's a side spreadsheet, more integrations on a product never built for this vertical doesn't close that gap.
