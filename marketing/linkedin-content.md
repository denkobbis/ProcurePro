# ProcurePro LinkedIn Content Pack

10 posts scripted for founder-led LinkedIn. Built from the demo script
(`marketing/demo-script.md`), the sales playbook, and real product behavior only —
**no fabricated social proof, testimonials, or customer counts** per `PRODUCT.md`.

Format rules for this audience (Nigerian ops/finance/procurement leads, read on a
phone between meetings):
- First line is the hook. If they scroll past it, done.
- Short paragraphs, no walls of text, at most ~120 words each.
- Every claim must be a claim the product can back up on a live demo.
- End with a specific, low-friction ask (comment, DM, or the demo form link).

Suggested visual in brackets on each post — use clips from `marketing/capture-demo.mjs`
footage or landing-page mockups (`/landing/*`). Never use a mocked testimonial or
a logo wall you don't have.

## Post 1 — The problem hook (Week 1)

> Nigerian procurement runs on trust, not process.
>
> A ₦2M order moves from a WhatsApp message to a "sent you a mail, please approve"
> to a spreadsheet nobody updated — and if the one person who knows the status is
> on leave, nobody knows the status.
>
> The spreadsheets aren't the problem. The problem is that nothing *enforces* the
> process when it counts: who approved, what budget it hit, whether the vendor's
> certificate expired last month.
>
> We're building ProcurePro so that question never has to be asked in a meeting again.
>

## Post 3 — The cost of the spreadsheets (Week 1)

> "We use a shared sheet" is the most expensive four words in Nigerian procurement.
>
> Because the sheet tracks *what you know to write down*. It can't tell you:
> - which ₦3M request has been sitting with an approver for 13 days
> - whether the Naira moved 45% against an open USD order
> - that two vendors share a bank account
>
> Enforced approval chains + budget checks + landed cost make those three stop being
> surprises. It's not about the sheet — it's about the visibility the sheet can't give.
>
> [Visual: the "Needs you today" flagged-rows mockup]

## Post 4 — Approval chains 101 (Week 1)

> Who approves what in your company today?
>
> If the answer is "whoever's around when the money's needed," your approvals aren't
> a control — they're theatre.
>
> Real approval chains have three properties: they're multi-step, they're configured
> (department + amount decide the path), and they're auditable. Every step recorded,
> every step someone can actually trace six months later.
>
> We built exactly that into ProcurePro — set it in a settings screen, change it
> yourself, no ticket to our team. Your rulebook, not ours.
>
> [Visual: the approval-rules table mockup — department/range/step/role]

## Post 5 — The NCDMB angle (Week 2)

> "Put it in the vendor file."
>
> Every oil & gas procurement officer has said it. The problem: the vendor file is a
> folder that lives in someone's inbox, and nobody re-reads a folder before a payment.
>
> NCDMB local content and certificate expiry belong **on the vendor record itself** —
> the same screen where you raise the PO against them — with a warning when a cert is
> about to lapse.
>
> Compliance shouldn't require remembering to check. It should be a field the system
> watches for you.
>
> [Visual: vendor record mockup with "71% local — NCDMB compliant" rows]

## Post 6 — Landed cost, not sticker price (Week 2)

> A PO is never just a PO when it crosses a border.
>
> $45,000 of valves isn't $45,000. It's $45,000 + freight + customs duty, and if the
> Naira moved after you priced it, that's real money on top.
>
> Most tools show you the sticker price and call it a day. ProcurePro rolls freight,
> customs, and FX into the landed cost on the PO — and flags the moment the currency
> moves against an open order.
>
> "How much is this *really* costing us?" should have a number, not a shrug.
>
> [Visual: landed-cost stat-plate mockup — PO value / freight / customs]

## Posting cadence

- Weeks 1–2: 4 posts/week (Mon/Wed/Thu/Fri), stagger ~8am Lagos time.
- Weeks 3–4: 3 posts/week (Tue/Thu/Sat).
- Between posts 5 and 6, run the paid LinkedIn boost on whichever post had the
  highest engagement — target Lagos/PHC, titles: Procurement, Supply Chain, Finance,
  Operations + "oil & gas".
- Every post links to the landing page with `?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social`
  so the UTM fields on `marketing_leads` attribute the lead.
- After post 10, replace the [X]s with real numbers and keep the cadence at 2/week.
  The moment you have a live customer, PRIORITY: one honest "we shipped for a real
  company" post + ask for permission for a testimonial.
